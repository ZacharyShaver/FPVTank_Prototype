Scorpion Head Options

Your call, 2026-11-07

"We might just have to keep it as a pin hole in the front. Later on a tail camera is going to be a must." So: a fixed pinhole camera in the nose (the last option below), the pan-tilt head off the body for now, and a camera on the tail later.

Where it comes out

  1. Only two options keep the head from landing first in a roll: roll hoops, and a fixed camera with no head. Every other spot puts the head, or its dome, on the outside of the robot's roll outline.
  2. If looking around with the head matters, use two roll hoops. The hoops land first, end over end and sideways, and the camera still sees the ground mm ahead. It stands mm taller than the open head. Seal the ball itself (below) and no dome is needed.
  3. If a fixed view is enough, a wide camera in the nose is the simplest. It wins all three readings (the body lands first, it sees right to the front, and it's the lowest), with no moving parts and a flat window. But it drops the head.
  4. The dome reads worst for the camera. It still lands first, and its clamp ring hangs over the nose like a brim, so the ground close in front is hidden.

Sealing the ball without a dome: the M12 lens's front is glass, so an O-ring round the lens barrel makes the lens the window. The light sensor only needs brightness, so a skin 2–3 layers thick of natural filament, printed as part of the ball, covers it. A TPU wiper at the pan joint keeps out dust.

How these were read

Lands first is whatever sits on the convex outline the robot rolls on, over its top, in the side view (end over end) and the front view (sideways). The tail is left out because it moves. The blind ground is the nearest ground ahead of the robot's front-most point that a line from the lens reaches without passing through the body's centre-line cut or a guard. It assumes the head can tilt down as far as it needs. The tyres sit off to the sides, so they don't block it.

None of the guards, the dome, the shelf, the stinger mount or the fixed camera is in the sims yet. The 250 mm run models the open head on top.
