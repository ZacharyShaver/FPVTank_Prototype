Can the Body Be the Roll Hoop?

Not by shape alone, under 250 mm

    body and tail tyres the 250 mm box the shell it would need ⊕ centre of mass pinhole camera

    A shell that rolls it back by itself

    The tail rolls it back

    Good

    • The body stays as it is: no hoops, no big shell
    • You want a tail anyway (and a camera on it later)
    • It's how T3 reads upright in every fall

    Against

    • Only works if the tail works: a jammed or broken tail leaves it on its back
    • A camera on the tail takes the push
    • Takes a second or two each time

    Invertible: either side up

    Good

    • Nothing to right: it drives off whichever way it lands
    • No tail needed for it, and nothing sticks up to break
    • A pinhole in the nose works either way up: flip the image in software (drawn high in the nose here; centred, it would see the same both ways)

    Against

    • Bigger wheels, a thin slab of a body: the parts inside just fit
    • A tail, and its camera, have to work both ways up
    • Lower clearance than the box, both ways up
    • The sims' falls gauge counts only one side as upright; it would need to count both

    How these were read

    The shell rule is the rugged body's: if the body's outline gets farther from the centre of mass all the way from the tyres' bottom edges to its top, the only place it can settle is on its wheels. The dashed circle is the smallest such outline round these wheels. The search tried it too: 600 shapes of the skin's top, and a dome up to 241 mm tall, inside the cap. None got past 26 % of starting orientations rolling back by shape alone.
