What each knob does to the numbers

Every knob of T3's design swept one at a time across its search range, on all seven gauges (G6 crest and G7 push are new and proposed), with the hills read with the tail stowed and braced: 602 screened designs, a re-read of the braced hills over five tail angles, and a side-slope probe. Five knobs are new: the tyres' width and grip, the tail mount's height, the tail foot's size, and a push face. The six candidate knobs from the first pass are worked through below.

The base design, T3

Read this first

  • Raise the tail mount over what it pushes. T3 pushes the worst of the five (1 kg) because its 150 mm tail mount catches a 150 mm box in reverse. At 170 mm it pushes 4 kg with the step intact, for 5° of braced hill. A push face on the nose does as well on the box but kills the step (30 mm).
  • Grip is the hill lever on ordinary ground. On x1 and x0.65 floors the hills end in a slide; 1.5x the tread's grip adds 5-10°. On grippy ground (x2.5) it adds nothing. The model's tyre friction is a guess worth measuring.
  • A slow crawl climbs worse, because of the drive's speed loop. Its torque at the stall scales with the speed asked for; braced, T3 makes 30° at 0.15 m/s and 45° at 0.5. Wheel-speed feedback with an integral term is the fix, in the robot's code.
  • Body height cuts both ways. Below T3's 52 mm ride height the step goes (40 mm: a 50 mm step; 15 mm: 30), because the body's front meets the step's face before the tyres lift it. Above it, the hills go: at 90 mm, 30° / 35° stowed and 35° / 40° braced, against T3's 35° / 40° and 45° / 45°. T3 sits about 10 mm above the step's cliff.
  • Bracing the tail doesn't cancel height. From 15 to 90 mm of ride height the hills lose 10° both ways with the tail braced, against 15° up and 10° down stowed. Steeper tail angles don't win it back (checked over five, -75° to -15°).
  • Wheel width costs only the turn. Track 110 to 180 mm widens the turn from 393 to 485 mm (and landing upright drops to 0.87 at 180); tyres 25 to 80 mm wide, 382 to 441. The step and the hills don't move: tipping fore and aft doesn't depend on width. Width pays only across a slope, and only on grippy ground.
  • The wheelbase is the hill lever, and the step's enemy. A tyre gap of 15 to 150 mm takes the hills from 25° / 30° to 50° / 50° stowed, but the step falls from 150 mm at T3's 62 to 40 mm from 82 up, and the turn grows from 384 to 525 mm.
  • The stowed tail costs 10° uphill. Without a tail the same robot reads 45° / 45° (but lands upright 0.69 and climbs 40 mm). Braced, the tail gives it back. Stowed lower it helps the hills (50° / 55° braced at 20°) but the turn swells to 900 mm.
  • Hard floors under the step: gearing under about 1.8x (83 mm at 1.45x; at 0.4x it stalls on a 15° hill), a tail shorter than 260 mm, a push angle shallower than -37°. Under 170 mm of tail or 2 N·m of servo it can't right itself (upright 0.6).
  • Dead in their ranges: the skin's squareness (p), its top shape (qu), its height above the middle (cu) up to 180 mm, the packs' height, the motor's size from 0.75x to 2x. The screen's noise on T3 over 8 seeds: the step varies by 5 mm, nothing else moves. The crest is read once a setting and swings 25-40° by luck (below).
  • Six wheels read 55° / 57° stowed and 55° / 60° braced, the best hills of all, but step 37 mm, turn 623 mm and 790 g more.

Two new gauges: crest and push

Both proposed; both negative controls pass (the line under each table). G6 crest: a ridge with a rounded peak, each side 5° to 60° steep, crawled over at 0.15 m/s on grippy ground (x2.5), 20 tries a direction, the better one counting. At the step's 0.5 m/s, momentum carried every belly over and hid what the gauge is for. It reads high-centring, where the belly grounds and the wheels hang: the Box falls from 15° to 5° with its belly 15 mm lower, and from 15° to 10° with a 1.5x wheelbase. T3 doesn't high-centre; it tips over the peak. G7 push: a 200 × 260 × 150 mm box, 0.5 to 12 kg, shoved 0.5 m within 6 s at 0.3 m/s, forwards or backwards, read at the friction band's worst. T3 reads 1 kg, the worst of the five: at x1.5 its nose climbs the box, and in reverse its tail mount catches it. On ordinary ground it shoves 5 kg.

T3's crest is a coin flip. From 25° to 40° it crosses in 75-95% of tries, against the 90% a reading needs, so 20 tries read 25° or 40° by luck (the gauge's own read: 25°; the knob sweep's T3: 40°). Backwards it crosses 40° every time, but 25° only 34 times in 40. The crest column in the knob table swings the same way, so read it only where it drops to 20° or less and stays there over neighbouring settings: a tail foot of 23 mm or more, a tyre gap of 105 mm or more, gearing at 1.1x or less, a push face, no tail.

The candidate knobs, worked through

K1 Tyre grip: yes, the only hill lever on ordinary ground.The tread's friction, 0.5x to 1.8x of today's, on T3. On ordinary ground (x1) half grip reads 20° every way; today's, 35° / 40° stowed and braced; 1.5x, 35° / 40° stowed and 40° / 45° braced. On the band's slipperiest (x0.65) today's reads 25° / 30° and 1.5x reads 35° / 40°: 10° from tread alone. The step needs at least today's grip (half: 20 mm; 0.93x: 150 mm). On grippy ground (x2.5) nothing moves: the robot tips there before it slides. The model's tyre friction is a guess; a tilt test on his floor would measure it (the ramp angle where a robot with locked wheels starts to slide).
K2 Tail as a counterweight: no.No pose held across the tail's arc beats the brace at -30° (45° / 45°), and braced, uphill ends in a stall, not a tip: the wheels run out of torque before the robot runs out of balance, so moving weight forward has nothing to give.
K3 Brace angle per design: a rule, not a knob.The fixed angles are already best on most designs (-30° up on 175 of 191, -15° down on 177). Setting it by rule, the foot just on the ground, lands within one 5° step of the best on about 98% of them; the misses look like noise (T3 at the rule's -29.5° reads 40° against 45° at -30°). It matters only where the foot's geometry moves: the mount's height, the tail's length.
K4 Tail foot: keep the 12 mm ball.A 6 mm foot: 40° / 40° stowed, 40° / 45° braced (5° up gained stowed, 5° lost braced). From 29 mm up, the hills fall to 35° / 35° stowed and 40° / 40° braced, the turn grows to 408-432 mm, the crest to 20° (from 23 mm), and at 40 mm the step to 110 mm.
K5 Ride height that moves: probably not worth an actuator.Low (15 mm) reads 45° / 45° stowed and 45° / 50° braced, and pushes 4 kg; the step needs it at 52 mm or more. That is 5-10° of hill for a lift mechanism, and the push comes cheaper from the tail mount (K7).
K6 Hill speed: the lever is the speed loop, not the speed.Slower is worse uphill: T3 braced reads 30° at 0.15 m/s against 45° at 0.5 (0.8 matches 0.5). The drive is modelled as a DC motor on a duty-cycle command with a proportional speed term, so asking for less speed gives less torque at the stall. Wheel-speed feedback with an integral term would let a crawl climb like a run. That is a controller change for the robot's code, not the CAD.
K7 Push face, new: no; raise the tail mount instead.A 60 mm plate across the nose (75 g) takes the worst push from 1 to 4 kg, but the step falls from 143 to 30 mm (60-80 with the plate's top at 125-150) because the plate meets the step's face. The fix is at the back: in reverse, T3's tail mount (150 mm up, the box's own height) catches the box's top edge and levers the robot up. With the mount at 170 mm, the worst push is 4 kg at 20 tries (5 kg on ordinary ground), and the step stays at 150 mm, for 5° of braced hill (40° / 40°). Control: a 200 mm box catches the 170 mm mount again (0.5 kg at x1.5). The rule: nothing on the rear face below the top of what it pushes.

Body height

Two knobs set it: ride height (the belly's height; body, insides and tail mount move together) and the skin's middle height. Ride height trades the step against the hills; the skin's middle height has a narrow band where the step works, and T3 sits in it.

Wheel width

Two knobs: the track (the wheels' centre planes from the centre line) and the tyres' own width, new in this sweep (46 mm in every design so far). Neither moves the step or the hills. The side-slope probe below is the one place width shows: across a slope, on grippy ground.

Knobs and tests to try next

The first pass's six knobs (tyre grip, the tail as a counterweight, brace angle per design, the tail foot, a moving ride height, hill speed) are worked through above as K1-K6, and its two recommended tests are built as G6 crest and G7 push.

Knobs

  1. Tail mount height in the searchesFree across 90-210 mm, now that it sets the push. The searches held it at 150.
  2. A rear push plateA flat face behind the tail mount, up to its height, so taller loads can't catch it. Costs mass at the back, where the hills don't mind it.
  3. Speed loop gainsAn integral term in the sim's drive, to read the hills as the robot would drive them with feedback.

Tests

  1. Step down and ledge dropDoes it nose-dive and flip coming off a ledge? Unchecked since the sharp-edge lesson.
  2. Rough groundA random heightfield (rubble, grass clumps): general mobility, where ride height and wheel size should pay.
  3. StairsG2's ladder stops at 150 mm; stairs are about 180 mm risers, one after another.
  4. Side slope (probed here)T3 55° at x2.5, 40° at x1 (a slide). Worth a gauge only if traverses matter; on ordinary ground it reads the tyres.
  5. Crest as a pass rateThe share of tries over a 30° ridge, rather than a ladder: smoother where a design sits on a plateau, as T3 does.

A Gaussian-process surrogate

Yes, and it already works for two of the gauges. The fitter is written and trains on what the searches already simulated, with no new sims. On the hills run (5 knobs, 712 designs) it predicts both hills to within 1.5-1.6° (the gauge reads in 5° steps) and the turn to 13 mm. Trained on the first even spread alone, it ranks the search's later designs at 0.92-0.94 rank correlation. On the shape study (10 knobs) the turn holds (7 mm) and the step weakens. On the body search (24 knobs, few spread designs) the step fails: it has cliffs, like the nose cliff, that a smooth model blurs. Landing upright can't be predicted among designs that already pass the screen: they differ by a few hundredths, about the screen's own noise. Its 95% bands hold 90-95% of the time. Good for: choosing which designs to simulate in a search (the ones it predicts are good or is unsure of), on the hills and the turn; and a map of which knobs matter across the whole range (last column), where the table above shows only T3's neighbourhood. Not for: final readings, the step, or upright.
