Seven gauges, iterations 1 and 2

Each gauge is a fixed test that reads one number off any design. G1–G3 and G7 read under three floor frictions (×0.65, ×1, ×1.5) and count the worst; the hills and the crest read on grippy ground (×2.5), where the design tips before its tyres slide. The big number is the best design; the box is the baseline to beat.

G1 Upright after a fall

Share of 200 falls from 300 mm, in random orientations, that end on the wheels. Target: at least 75 %.

The scorecard

The box is the built Trainer's parts and masses in a plain box on the Trainer's wheels: same weight and balance, plain shape. Box, Trainer, Step layout and Six-wheel come from chassis-v2's built meshes; the E4 weeble is the drop-test shell over the sweep's robot. Rows marked search are the shape study's finalists (T3 is the design the targets were set against); the row marked knob is T3 with its tail mount raised to 170 mm, the knob log's fix for the push. A reading in green meets its target (upright 75 %, crest 20°, push 3 kg). A hill reading marked braced is with the tail held down behind as a wheelie bar.

The checks behind the readings

A gauge is read only when its controls pass: a design known to be worse has to read worse. The step edge check guards the fix for a sharp edge that acted as a wall. The hills, crest and push controls were read in the same pass as their readings above.

How each gauge is read

  1. Physics band. A hard floor with the ball's measured bounce, 0.2 ms time steps, every friction scaled ×0.65, ×1 and ×1.5. The 37 mm gearmotor's numbers are datasheet guesses; each side's speed is held by its encoders.
  2. G1 Upright after a fall. 200 falls per friction, each from a uniformly random orientation with its lowest point 300 mm up and a spin of up to 3 rad/s, wheels unpowered. The rest state is the body axis nearest world up. Controls: the E4 reads at least 0.2 above the box, and the E4 with 1 kg on its roof reads below the E4.
  3. G2 Step up. Steps of 10 to 150 mm with a 3 mm round on the top edge. The robot starts 80 mm short, within 10° of square, at 0.5 m/s, and tries forwards and in reverse. A try succeeds when the robot is on top and on its wheels within 4 s. Control: the box on 70 mm wheels reads below the box.
  4. G3 Turn-around space. From rest, one side backward and the other forward at 60 % of no-load speed, both ways, until it has turned 180° (6 s at most). The circle is centred where its wheels' centre started and holds every point of the body and tyres. Control: the box stretched 1.5× reads above the box.
  5. G4 Hill up, G5 Hill down. The floor with gravity tipped by the slope, friction ×2.5, from rest, the set speed (0.5 m/s) ramped up over 1 s, the better direction counting. It fails if it tips (past 60° from the slope's normal, or 30° at the end) or, coming down, skids. The reading is the better of the tail stowed and held at −30°, −15° or 0° (a design may use its limbs); stowed, no reading may beat the static tip angle. Control: T3 with 1 kg high on its top reads below T3 both ways.
  6. G6 Crest. Two planks at ± the angle meeting at a peak 150 mm up, rounded by 3 mm; crawling at 0.15 m/s with the step's tail reflex, friction ×2.5. It passes with its centre of mass 0.1 m past the far foot, upright. T3 reads on a plateau: from 25° to 40° it crosses in 75–95 % of tries, so 20 tries read it 25° or 40° by luck. Other designs do too: read again with fresh seeds (2026-10-28), the Trainer went from 10° to 20°, the step layout from 25° to 35° and T3 from 25° to 40°, while the box and the six-wheel held at 15°. Treat a crest difference under 15° as noise. Controls: the box with its belly 15 mm lower, and the box 1.5× long, read below the box.
  7. G7 Push. A box 200 mm deep, 260 wide and 150 tall on the floor's own friction, pushed 0.5 m at 0.3 m/s within 6 s, square on ±10°, tail stowed, the better direction counting. It fails if the robot tips or rides 40 mm up the box. Controls: T3 geared 0.4× reads below T3; T3 at half tread grip reads below T3 at ×0.65.
  8. Trust. Only G1 has been checked against real drops, and only on printed bodies. The other six ride on tyre grip, which hasn't been measured on a real floor yet, and the tail's 3.3 N m servo is a size Zach doesn't own yet.
