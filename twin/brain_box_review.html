The calls, with the picks you took

C1 · sets the box's size

How many packs ride in the box: two or three?

  • Built today: two, one per ESC (each drive side on its own pack).
  • The power plan: two drive packs on a selector switch, plus pack C for the Pi. That's three in the robot, and untethered.
  • The sims carry two. A third 5200 is 141 × 46 × 26 mm and 300 g.

Resolved: two. One runs both ESCs, the other runs the Pi through the Pololu, so the robot comes off the wall. A third 5200 wouldn't fit anyway: 141 mm against 126 across the back.

C2 · the kill switch

Which switch is the e-stop?

  • The sims have a separate red e-stop button on the side.
  • The power plan's Carling selector has a centre-off, which kills the drive.
  • Each INJORA ESC also has its own power switch on a lead.

Resolved: a latching button in the wire from GPIO 17 to the PCA's OE, recessed but within reach. The Pi can trigger the same line and reads the button on GPIO 27. The ESC switches stay on, tucked inside, as the hard power cut.

C3 · Pi power

Does the Pi get its own switch?

  • The plan: the Pololu 5 V regulator, and no switch, so the Pi boots when pack C is plugged in.
  • The sims have a Pi power switch and a 45 mm UBEC, the plan's superseded alternative.

Resolved: the Pololu, with a small switch from its EN pin to ground that puts the board to sleep. It carries almost no current, unlike an inline switch. Bench-test it first: Pololu doesn't say what a floating EN does.

C4 · servo power, for the tail

What powers the tail's servos?

  • Today the servos run off the left ESC's 5 V / 2 A BEC (built for two MG90Ds).
  • The tail the sims search uses servos of 2–3.5 N·m stall, which draw several amps.
  • The sims have an 8 A UBEC for it, which isn't in the plan or on the shopping list.

Resolved: nothing bought now. A 48 × 30 × 15 slot (the sims' size, a guess) stays open at the back of the box, and the BEC comes with the tail's servos.

C5 · missing part

Buy an IMU?

  • The sims carry one near the centre of mass (a BNO085, guessed size). The tail's righting reflex reads it to know it's upside down.
  • It also lets a real run log pitch, roll and heading, to set beside the sim's. That's useful for the pitch before there's a tail.
  • Its address is 0x4A, clear of the PCA9685's 0x40. But Adafruit lists the chip as troublesome on I²C: it breaks the bus's timing and sometimes needs a reset.
  • The PCA9685 is on that bus. If the bus hangs, the PCA keeps sending its last throttle and the Pi can't change it. Only OE or the stop button still stops the motors.

Resolved: buy it now, and run it in its serial mode (UART-RVC): one signal wire to Pi pin 10, off the motors' bus. You get yaw, pitch, roll and acceleration, but no raw gyro.

C6 · the Pi's pack gauge

The INA219 and the PCA9685 both want address 0x40. And when does the INA219 get bought?

  • The clash is one solder bridge: A0 moves the INA219 to 0x41.
  • It's how the Pi sees its own pack and stops cleanly. The ESCs have a cutoff for the drive pack. The Pololu has none, and the alarm only beeps.
  • If nobody hears the alarm, the Pi keeps draining its pack to about 5.9 V, or 2.95 V a cell, below the 3.0 V floor.
  • It was parked as "later" when the Pi was tethered, and it stayed there when the Pi got its own pack.

Resolved: buy it in this order ($9.95). Wire it and write the shutdown code once the robot drives. The picture and wiring are just below.

What the INA219 is, for C6

What it's for: it lets the Pi's software read how full its own pack is. Today only the alarm knows, and the alarm can only beep. Think of it as a health endpoint for the battery: a number the program can act on.

What it's doing:

  1. The pack's + wire runs through a 0.1 Ω resistor on the board, so all the Pi's current passes through it.
  2. The chip measures the voltage across that resistor, which gives the current, and the voltage at its far end, which gives the pack's level.
  3. The Pi asks for both numbers over I²C, about once a second.
  4. At 7.0 V, a program stops the motors and then shuts the Pi down. That program doesn't exist yet.

What it doesn't do: it never cuts power, so if the program isn't running, nothing happens. It only watches the Pi's pack. The ESCs' own cutoff covers the drive pack.

What it costs: $9.95, four thin wires, and that program. The resistor also loses about 0.2 V at 2 A, so the Pololu holds 5 V down to about 6.1 V in, not 5.9. That's still well under the alarm.

Check before wiring:

  • INA219's power pin: Adafruit's newer boards may print it as VIN, not VCC. It's the header pin that takes 3 to 5 V. Vin+ on the terminal block is the pack wire. Mixing them up puts 8.4 V on the chip.
  • Pololu's holes: VOUT and GND each carry several wires here. The board duplicates those pins, but I don't have the hole count. If they run out, splice.
  • Guesses: the 1 kΩ on pin 13 (*), and P0 tied to the IMU's own 3Vo, where Adafruit uses the Pi's 3 V.

C7 · radio

WiFi dongle and antenna: in or out?

  • The sims have a USB dongle with an antenna up a hoop.
  • The plan doesn't. The Pi 5 has its own WiFi, and a closed box with a lid round it cuts range.
  • Measured 2026-07-14, with the Pi inside the old chassis: only about 2.5-5 Mbps. A 720p30 stream reached the desktop at 1.5-7.6 fps. Small packets were fine, at 4-14 ms.
  • The cable that fixed this (31.9 fps at 4.4 Mbps) goes away once the robot is off the wall, so WiFi becomes the only link.
  • Measured again 2026-11-08, power save off, against a bar of 6 Mbps: 180-188 Mbps on 5 GHz and 41-45 on 2.4 GHz in the open, and 47-48 on 2.4 GHz inside PLA.

Resolved: no dongle. Leave a USB port reachable and the lid radio-clear (PLA is) over the Pi. Not tested yet: packs on each side, and the far end of the driving area, so rerun it there on the first drive.

C8 · the IR lights

Where do the two IR LED pods go?

  • You own them (the camera kit). They flanked the camera in the head.
  • The sims put lights on the claws.
  • With a pinhole in the nose, the head is gone.

Resolved: on the tail, with the Arducam IR camera, when the tail comes. So the first robot has no IR. The nose gets the IMX219 120° camera you own, and the pinhole lens is off the list.

C9 · heat

A sealed box against cooling the Pi.

  • The drop test wants a closed lid with pads, and dust wants it sealed.
  • The Pi 5 runs up to ~12 W at full load (YOLO on the Hailo too), and the Pololu and ESCs add heat.
  • Rough sum: 15 W in a sealed 250 × 140 × 50 box sits about 20-25 °C over the room. PLA starts to soften near 55-60 °C, so that leaves little margin, and the Pi's cooler would be breathing that warm air.

Resolved: plain vent slots in and out, past the Pi box's own vents, with the lid's pads clear of them. No filter, to keep it simple; add one only if dust becomes a problem. Then run a load test in the printed box.

C10 · getting at the packs

A screwed lid against charging and swapping the packs.

  • The fitted design holds the packs under a screwed lid.
  • With the selector gone, there's no flipping to a second pack: a swap means opening up. You own 4, so 2 can sit charged and ready.
  • The drop test modelled one lid over everything. Doors haven't been tested.

My pick: one screwed lid over everything for v2. That replaces the separate battery door I first proposed: the door is two more parts and more screws. The stop button and Pi switch go on the shell, so only a pack swap needs the lid off. Charge the packs out of the robot. Add a door later if swaps get annoying. Your call is pending.

C11 · encoders

Who counts the new motors' encoders?

  • Four JGB37-520s with Hall encoders: 8 signal wires plus power.
  • The Pi's GPIO in Python may not keep up.

Resolved: your ESP32-WROOM-32 reads all four with its 8 pulse counters, so no Pico. It goes beside the PCA.
