Modular Floor

Your idea: one base, and a small printed holder for each part, so a bad fit costs one small reprint instead of a whole body. Checked against your real parts, the slicer and the drop sim.

Keep the idea. One base with cheap holders takes a fit change from 7 h 00 to 6-24 minutes. And there are lots of ways to lay it out, as you said. Real 42 mm cells fit in two full layers, though the body grows to about 134 mm tall against 78.

My pick is smaller still: a floor of holes, with a low frame round each part. It fits on one level, adds about 55 g, and kept every part within 0.5 mm over 80 sim drops. The same floor also takes different layouts, so choosing one isn't final. Weight isn't the deciding problem, and you don't need snaps, because the lid does the holding.

What full-size cells cost

A Gridfinity holder is always a whole number of cells, so every part rounds up. On one floor with the packs on the walls, pack, Pi box, pack is 118 mm of parts but 168 mm on the grid, against 134 mm inside the body. I first called that "doesn't fit". That only holds for one floor and the sim's layout: two full layers take the 29 cells in 30, but they stand about 134 mm tall.

On hold. Holders come first: nothing here matters until each part stays in its holder and each holder stays in the floor.

All four fit a 250 × 140 footprint as holes and frames, checked for collisions and against your wire lengths. Blue parts sit on a shelf or deck, and the rest stand on the floor. These are rough layouts for comparing, and only A has a sim behind it.

The wires moved three of these. A pack's lead leaves by its end, and I'd drawn that end about 3 mm from an ESC. So A's ESCs moved 19.5 mm back, and B's and C's packs moved 20 mm forward. C's deck also came off its ESCs: lying flat, their leads come out of the top, with 3 mm to the deck. That took C from 201 to 249 mm long.

Nothing is out of reach, and A has wire to spare. One pack feeds both ESCs, so its 120 mm lead plus an ESC's 90 mm red and black gives 210 mm, before the Y-harness adds more. A uses 71 and 148 of that, so about 62 to 139 mm a run has to be tucked away, and there's room over the ESCs while the box stays 78 mm tall. D uses 173 and 202, so almost nothing to tuck. Each ESC feeds two motors, so its yellow and blue get spliced anyway, and past 105 mm is just more of the 18 AWG wire on the list.

Your guess on weight

Right direction, small effect. The sim's T3 reads almost the same at 5 % heavier, which is more than Gridfinity would add, and with its center of mass 3 mm higher, which is more than either would move it. Upright goes 1.00 to 1.00 and 0.96, the step stays at 150 mm, and the turn goes 448 to 459 and 444 mm. Those are one design's readings. Height costs more in the box itself: its stack is already too tall for the lid, and Gridfinity would add up to 6 mm.

Where it helps and hurts

Helps

  • Fit changes. Print 1 came back with every fit loose. With holders, a second try costs 2 h 10 in all, and the base stays.
  • Parts you haven't measured. The PCA, ESP32, IMU and alarm can start from a guess, because a wrong guess costs minutes.
  • Balance you can adjust on the robot. T3's step falls from 150 to 57 mm with its center of mass 5 mm forward. Moving one pack 15 mm shifts it about 1 mm, so the sim's pack knob becomes a real one.
  • The same insides in different bodies. Any body with this hole pattern takes the same holders, and every design in the search carries the same parts.
  • Repairs. A broken holder is a small reprint.

Hurts

  • Pegs take the side loads. A pack hit its frame with 868 N (90th percentile, 1 m onto hard ground, no tyres). That's about 217 N a peg with four 5 mm pegs, or 11 MPa. I don't know what your printed pegs can take, so this gets tested first.
  • Thin walls may not last. The sim treats frames as rigid. At 1.2 mm, the walls round the packs may need to be thicker.
  • It needs a flat floor. Print 1's tunnel ridge gives two levels. Putting the Pi on the floor also helps the box's height problem.
  • Holes open the floor. Dirt can get in from below, and blind holes would add about 1 mm of floor. The hole patch also takes 21 minutes longer to print.
  • The first build takes longer. Add 2 h 10 of holders on top of the base.

Snap, friction or lid?

Withdrawn: this isn't a result. The drop test welds every frame to the floor, so the pegs never get pulled. Its lid presses through solid rubber columns it counts at 1 g each; solid, they'd be about 109 g in all. With the lid over a Pi on a flat floor, the ESC's column would be about 39 mm tall and the PCA's about 60. So what holds each part is still open, and C10 with it. What follows is kept for the record.

The lid. I dropped the same frames with no lid as a control. Over 80 drops the Pi ended out of place 76 times, the PCA 72, and the packs 19 and 21. With the lid's TPU pads, none did. Those pads saw up to 392 N per pack, which I wouldn't expect a printed snap or friction fit to hold. One Gridfinity latch design has a user report that PLA deforms under the constant load, and standard Gridfinity only holds by gravity or magnets.

So let the pegs stop sideways movement and the lid stop everything lifting. A slight friction fit is still handy for keeping holders in place while the lid is off. This also backs one lid for C10.

Not tested, and the guesses

  • Not tested: how strong printed pegs are, how a frame flexes, or base warp over a long frame. The sim's ESC is a plain box with no wires, so it can't see them wedging the ESC in an open frame, as yours does on the bench. The sim's hard ground with no tyres is harsher than the robot will see.
  • Gridfinity's foot under side loads: not simulated. Its 45° taper could push a holder upward against the lid.
  • My picks: holes every 15 mm, 1.2 mm walls, 0.25 mm round each part, walls at about half each part's height.
  • Your bench: a 5 mm peg wants a 5.1 mm hole. My 5.3 fell out when tipped.
  • Wire guesses: 15 mm of straight lead off a pack's end, 10 off an ESC, a pack's lead leaving by its short end, and where the motors' wires come in. The first robot's axles aren't drawn yet, so those are T3's. The Y-harness's length is left out.
  • Sizes not measured: PCA, ESP32, servo BEC, alarm and IMU. Gridfinity's foot profile comes from secondary sources, and I couldn't open the official spec file.
