Why it rolls back upright

Lying on any face, the robot stays only if its centre of mass sits over that face. The skirts and both hoops lie on one outline round the centre of mass. From the tracks' bottom edges up to the top, that outline only gets farther from the centre of mass. So whatever it lands on, gravity rolls it towards the tracks, the nearest part of the outline. The left chart is that outline seen head-on, which is what it rolls on; the right one is the belly.

Where it can come to rest after a tumble

Stable faces of the whole robot's outline (its convex hull), with the tilt needed to knock it off each. Faces it leaves with under 1.5° of tilt are mesh slivers, not rests, and are left out.

The new parts

  • Roll hoops, front and rear: Ø13 tube on the outline, TPU sleeves on top. The front one stands behind the head, out of the cradle's sweep. The rear one leans back so it won't stand on its tail, and carries the WiFi antenna inside its top.
  • Brow bar: from the front hoop's top over the ball, so a nose-dive lands on it and not on the camera.
  • Side skirts: armour outside the tracks, on the outline, 7–15 mm from the outer frames. They roll it off its side, take side hits and keep thrown dirt off the top. Their ends sweep up, so they don't set the approach or departure angle.
  • TPU bumpers where it lands: hoop tops, rub strips on the skirts, claw caps, tail corners.
  • Shock: the Pi on 4 TPU isolators; TPU-faced clamp bars hold the packs down; the cargo bed is screwed on, not held by magnets.
  • Layout: the packs stand on edge in bays along the walls, 73 mm further forward, beside the head. The ESCs come down from the sponsons to the floor behind them. The deck is 15 lower, and the whole body sits 15 further forward on the legs.
  • Belly: a tunnel down the middle, its roof 47 over the ground. The nose floor is raised 10 with the head, and a 28° ramp at the tail. Packs lift out from the top: carapace and two turret-deck side lids off.
  • Dust: a gasket under the bed and along the tub-to-carapace seam, a TPU wiper ring from the deck onto the drum, boots round the motor windows, grommets where the head's leads pass. Air for the Pi's cooler comes in high through a foam-filtered hood on the tail and leaves through louvres facing aft under the overhang.

Fit checks

Overlap in 1 mm cubes. Zero is clear; each control must read above zero, or the check would be blind.

Guesses to confirm

  • Masses: printed walls 90 % solid, hoop tubes 45 %, lumps 40 %; packs 300 g each, head 210 g. The outline is sized from the centre of mass, so weigh the real parts when they exist.
  • A printed Ø13 hoop may crack in a hard landing. A 10 mm aluminium or carbon tube inside it is the safe version.
  • ESC 46 × 34 × 18, on edge in a sealed bay: its heat needs the air path. Arm UBEC 50 × 30 × 15.
  • TPU isolators and gaskets: Shore 95A, the TPU you print tracks with.

Questions for you

  • Is the cage size OK? Rolling back upright on its own needs the top about 207 over the ground and the skirts 270 wide. A smaller cage only protects, and leaves the righting to the flippers next round.
  • The hoops and the brow bar cross the head camera's view up and back, and up and ahead. Thin, and above the lens, but there.
  • Packs now come out the top (carapace and side lids off), not the back. OK?
  • Climbing: the v1 track's flat contact is only 77 mm long, and that sets the tip angles. The arm at the tail costs 12°. The bigger tracks would raise all of it.
