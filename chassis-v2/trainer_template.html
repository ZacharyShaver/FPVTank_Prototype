Scorpion Trainer: on wheels

For training, at least for now: the rugged body on four wheels, with the tracks and the fenders over them gone. Every corner is the same module: a gearmotor in a printed pod on the hull wall, and a dish wheel wrapped back over it. In a simulator the whole drive is four hinges and four tyre contacts.     Built for the simulator

    • Rigid: no suspension, so the robot is one body on four hinges. Springs would need tuning twice, once in the simulator and once on the floor.
    • Four identical corners: one motor model, one tyre model, one set of numbers to fit.
    • An encoder on every wheel: the real wheel speeds can be logged and the simulator's motor fitted to them. Wheel slip shows up as encoders that disagree.
    • Tyre grip: measure it once with a tilt test (the robot on a board, raised until it slides) and put that number in the simulator. That's the main thing a simulator gets wrong about skid steering.
    • Tracks, by comparison: ~120 bodies and ~70 contacts in the v2 design (46 links a side in a closed loop, 9 wheels, 5 sprung arms). Simulators fake tracks, and the fake doesn't match the real track.

    Where it can come to rest after a tumble

    Stable faces of the whole robot's outline, with the tilt needed to knock it off each; faces it leaves with under 1.5° are mesh slivers. Neither has the cage or the arm.

    Shopping list

    Fit checks

    Overlap in 1 mm cubes, part against part. Zero is clear; each control must read above zero, or the check would be blind.

    Guesses to confirm

    • The gearmotor (the v2 legs' choice, now four): Ø37 gearbox 22 long, motor Ø34.5 × 33, encoder 11, 6 mm D shaft 15.5 long, face holes on a 31 mm circle, 205 g. Buy one and caliper it: only the pod's tube and lip change.
    • Its speed at 7.4 V: 200 rpm, which gives on these wheels. A slower ratio (50:1) suits a careful learner better.
    • The tyre: Ø110 × 46, printed TPU with 3.5 mm chevron lugs, . Its grip on dirt and floor is unknown until the tilt test.
    • Rim and pod: ~120 g a corner (thin walls print solid). Spokes in the disc could save a third.
    • Axles at the ends of the hull wall's straight run (X 91 and −93), at the v2 sprocket's height (Z 30).
    • Masses as on the rugged page (walls 90 % solid, packs 300 g).

    Questions for you

    • Is 308 wide OK? The nose holds the head and the packs, so the motors sit outside the hull. Narrower options: worm gearmotors lying along the wall (~294); the front pair across a box ahead of the head, with the claws on top (~262 wide, ~40 longer); or two motors with a chain to the front wheels.
    • “En masse”: many copies in the simulator, several real robots, or both? If simulator, should I build this robot's MuJoCo model next (four wheels and the head, masses from these parts)?
    • What ground will it train on? Floor and grass suit 25 mm gearmotors (~100 g each, ~400 g lighter). Rocks and slopes want the 37 mm.
    • Drivers: each ESC drives its side's two motors in parallel, and the Pi counts the 4 encoders (8 pins). OK, or a 4-channel driver with encoder inputs?
    • Arm: the pedestal stays; no arm is fitted. One of A–E on the trainer, or none for now?
    • First print: one corner (pod, wheel, tyre) on the motor you buy.
