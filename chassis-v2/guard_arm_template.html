Scorpion: guard arm (option E)

An arm at the tail built to replace the roll cage, on the rugged body with v2 legs, the hoops, brow bar and skirts removed. It guards the head while driving, braces so the robot doesn't roll, and pushes it upright when it does. It still carries the camera and gripper. Pick a situation; click a part to name it. The arm at work

Righting from every place it can come to rest

The arm leaves the guard pose, the robot settles on its bare body, and the arm reaches the ground and pushes it over the edge towards upright, at 1.2× the force that balances it. The load is the worst servo's share of its stall torque; under 80 % passes.

The software: a tip monitor

  • Always: from the IMU's tilt, the arm's pose and the known centre of mass, it works out the degrees left before tipping in each direction, many times a second.
  • Margin shrinking: slow the tracks; bring the arm low and to the uphill side; refuse the periscope pose on a slope.
  • Before a step or a steep cross-slope: plant the brace, behind or on the downhill side.
  • Tipping anyway, or falling: stop the tracks and go to the guard pose (90° in ~0.25 s); the peg and the stops take the landing.
  • Landed: read which way is down, leave the guard, and run the righting move for that rest (the table on the left), then check it's upright.

Fit checks

Overlap in 1 mm cubes. Zero is clear; each control must read above zero, or the check would be blind.

Guesses to confirm

  • Servo stall torques at about 7 V: DS3235 35, DS3218 21 kg·cm; the N20 worm drive ~20 kg·cm at the turntable.
  • Arm masses: base with servos 225 g, links and tool 160 g, payload 100 g.
  • Links: upper arm 140, forearm 175, tool 62 mm. The guard pose and the reach come from these.
  • Righting assumes the jaw caps grip the ground (TPU) and ignores the robot's bounce. A bench test settles it.
  • The slip clutch's setting: about 40 kg·cm, just over the shoulder servo's stall.
