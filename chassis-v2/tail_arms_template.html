The body

  • Your v1 legs, unmodified, on hull.py's tub; the head low in the nose as before. Everything inside stays where the fit checks put it.
  • Crawler armour: a sloped glacis behind the head, chamfered sponsons with the ESCs over their motors, louvres; a roll hoop over the head (169 over the ground; the ball tops out at 151).
  • Workhorse cargo bed: the brain hatch, with a lip and four tie-down eyes, and a stow post the gripper holds while driving.
  • The tail rises 30° from the bed to a round pedestal at the back. The arm's yaw servo sits under it, and the power bay below gets 21 mm taller.
  • The WiFi antenna moves to the front of the left sponson, away from the arm. The arm gets its own 8 A UBEC in the left sponson behind its ESC.

How they compare

Numbers

Held torque: the worst over every pose, with 100 g in the jaws, against the servo's stall torque at 6 V*. Margin 2 or more is comfortable. Ground reach: jaw tip on the ground, jaws pointing down, the arm clear of the robot.

Fit checks

Overlap, counted in 1 mm cubes. Zero is clear; each control must read above zero, or the check would be blind.

Guesses to confirm

  • Stall torques at 6 V: MG996R 11, DS3218 21, MG90S 2 kg·cm (datasheets).
  • The tool: wrist + gripper + camera 60 g; payload 100 g.
  • The arm camera: a 25 × 24 board. USB, or the Pi 5's second CSI port?
  • Lazy-susan bearing r 32 under the turntable.
  • Printed parts weigh 35 % of solid; packs 300 g each.

Questions for you

  • Which arm, or which mix? E.g. A's parallelogram with B's low stow.
  • "Slope up": here it's the tail rising to the pedestal, and A and D stow sloping up like a tail. Did you mean the body, the arm at rest, or both?
  • Where should it pick things up? Beside the track (A, C), or behind (all four; D at one spot only)? A tracked robot can turn on the spot to put the thing behind it.
