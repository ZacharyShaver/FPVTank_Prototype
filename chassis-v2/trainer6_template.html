Scorpion Trainer on six wheels

The Trainer with a middle pair: the same corner module six times, three a side. Three Ø110 wheels don't fit along the hull wall, so these are Ø100 and the wheelbase grows from 184 to 216 mm, with the middle axle under the centre of mass. What it costs

Rough ground, from the side

Each robot is pushed across the ground in 4 mm steps. At each step it settles where its centre of mass sits lowest with nothing below the ground, so it rocks and pitches the way a rigid robot does. Its outline is sliced from every part, so the pods and pads count as well as the belly. Grip isn't modelled.

The module (×6, all the same)

    In the simulator

    • 7 bodies (the robot and six wheels), six hinges and six tyre contacts. The four-wheel Trainer has 5 bodies, and the tracks ~120.
    • Still rigid: on bumpy ground one wheel can lift. That's fine for training, because the simulator lifts the same wheel. A sprung or pivoting middle wheel would keep all six down, but it adds a joint to fit twice, once in the simulator and once on the floor.
    • Six identical modules: one motor model, one tyre model. Six encoders: a wheel that slips shows up as one that disagrees with the other two on its side.
    • Tyre grip is still the number to measure first: one tilt test, then put it in the simulator.

    Where it can come to rest after a tumble

    Stable faces of the whole robot's outline, with the tilt needed to knock it off each; faces it leaves with under 1.5° are mesh slivers. Neither has the cage or the arm.

    Fit checks

    Overlap in 1 mm cubes, part against part. Zero is clear; each control must read above zero, or the check would be blind.

    Questions for you

    • What do you want six wheels for? Crests and ledge edges, which this does well; gaps and steps, where it needs bigger wheels or a pivoting middle pair; or spare drive when a motor dies? The answer picks the next version.
    • Mass: Six 25 mm gearmotors (~95 g each*) would bring it back to about the four-wheel's mass. Each motor needs less torque here: see “On a hill”.
    • Rigid, or let the middle pair pivot? A pivoting beam per side (a rocker) keeps all six on the ground over bumps. It needs a bearing per side; you had 6704s.
    • Drivers: each ESC drives its side's three motors in parallel, and the Pi counts 6 encoders (12 pins). OK, or a 6-channel driver?
    • The front cheeks: the front pods sit past the end of the hull's straight wall, so their pads fill back to the slanted nose (part of the tub). OK, or move the wheels back and lose some ridge clearance?
    • First print: the same as before, one module on the motor you buy. Nothing here changes the module except the wheel's size.
