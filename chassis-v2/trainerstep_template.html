Scorpion Trainer, step layout

The Trainer re-laid for driving down steps, from the simulator's search (research/tumble/chassis3.py): Ø92 wheels on a 130 mm wheelbase (axles at X 53 and −77), and the belly 54 mm off the ground instead of 32. The axles now sit under the tub's floor, so each pod hangs from a drop plate screwed to the wall above the floor. The body, the head and the packs are unchanged. Trainer → step layout: what it gains

What it costs

Down a step, simulated from these meshes

Each robot drives off a step of 10–40 cm at 0.3–1.4 m/s, forwards and in reverse, 20 runs a cell. It counts as a success if it gets down and comes to rest on its wheels. The encoders hold the set speed; the floor is the hard floor the drop tests validated. The pods are their own contact shapes, so they can't bridge into a skid plate under the body. Going fast is still the safest way down for both.

Rough ground, from the side

Each robot is pushed across the ground in 4 mm steps. At each step it settles where its centre of mass sits lowest with nothing below the ground, so it rocks and pitches the way a rigid robot does. Its outline is sliced from every part, so the pods and drop plates count as well as the belly. Grip isn't modelled.

    In the simulator

    • The same 5 bodies as the Trainer: the robot and four wheels, four hinges, four tyre contacts. Only the numbers change.
    • Where the layout came from: about 140,000 simulated step runs over wheel size, axle positions, belly height and pack position, under length caps. This was the best at the Trainer's length.
    • What carries it: the short wheelbase and the raised belly together. Raising the belly alone did nothing (0.51). The Trainer's own Ø110 wheels on these axles did as well as Ø92 (0.65), so SCORPION_STEP_R=55 builds that version.
    • Not scored by the search: hills, tumbles and the head's exposure. They are under "What it costs", and some are worse.

    Where it can come to rest after a tumble

    Stable faces of the whole robot's outline, with the tilt needed to knock it off each; faces it leaves with under 1.5° are mesh slivers. Neither has the cage or the arm.

    Fit checks

    Overlap in 1 mm cubes, part against part. Zero is clear; each control must read above zero, or the check would be blind.

    Guesses to confirm

    • The gearmotor, unchanged: Ø37 gearbox 22 long, motor Ø34.5 × 33, encoder 11, 6 mm D shaft, face holes on a 31 mm circle, 205 g. Buy one and caliper it.
    • Its speed: 200 rpm at 7.4 V, on Ø92 wheels (Ø110: ). The step runs assumed 330 rpm, 1.0 N·m stall at the wheel*.

    Questions for you

    • Hills against steps: it now tips forward on a downhill (the Trainer: ). Is a steeper crest and a safer step worth that, or should the next search score hills too?
    • Ø92 or keep Ø110? The simulator scores them the same here. Ø110 keeps today's wheel and tyre, the top speed and the gap crossing; Ø92 is lighter and lower.
    • The pods hang below the floor, off the ground. They're inside the tyres' outline from the side, so a step's edge can't catch them, but a rock between the wheels can. OK?
    • The head is the first thing to land in more falls (the cradle: ). Is that a reason to bring the cage back, or to keep the guard arm?
    • First print: one drop plate and pod on a scrap of wall, to feel how stiff 5 mm is before printing four.
